01 Problem Discovery: The Fatal 90-Second Human Bottleneck

Highway freight transport in India suffers heavily from night-time fatigue crashes. While commercial dashcams and Driver Monitoring Systems (DMS) can detect microsleeps with high accuracy, the operational reaction time is broken.

When a camera detects drowsiness at 75 km/h, the current workflow sends a notification to a central dispatcher desk. In practice, a dispatcher handling dozens of screens took between 60 to 90 seconds to notice the alert, find the driver's phone number, and manually dial. At highway speed, a sleeping driver travels 1.8 kilometers in 90 seconds—turning preventable fatigue into fatal collisions.

02 In-Cabin Ergonomics & Acoustic Reality

I spent time researching real truck cab environments and interviewing long-haul drivers. Three crucial design constraints emerged:

  • Zero Visual Interaction: Requiring a driver to look at a smartphone or press a button while driving at night increases accident risk. The entire interaction had to be 100% verbal and hands-free.
  • Severe Cabin Acoustic Noise: Heavy-duty diesel engines, open windows, and road rumble generate 75–85 dB of ambient cabin noise, requiring specialized noise suppression before speech processing.
  • Vernacular Nuances: Over 85% of long-haul drivers communicate colloquially in Hindi or regional dialects. English-speaking AI voice assistants would create cognitive friction and alienation.

03 Product Architecture: Sub-Second WebRTC + Sarvam AI

Cellular phone calls (SIP/GSM) introduce 4 to 7 seconds of dialing handshake latency. To achieve sub-second response times, we built on WebRTC over LiveKit:

Ultra-Low Latency Audio: LiveKit WebRTC persistent data channels allow bidirectional audio streaming with under 180ms network latency.
Sarvam AI Indic Models: State-of-the-art Hindi voice models optimized for colloquial dialect comprehension and natural synthesized speech in sub-400ms.

04 Deterministic State Machines vs LLM Unpredictability

In life-critical safety workflows, you cannot allow an LLM to hallucinate or get stuck in conversational tangents. As PM, I mandated a finite state machine (FSM) governing the workflow:

  1. State 1: Alert Triggered: Camera flags drowsiness. LiveKit immediately connects audio.
  2. State 2: Voice Check Initiated: Agent speaks fixed prompt in natural Hindi.
  3. State 3: Acknowledgment Gate: Driver speaks. Acoustic classifier checks for alertness keywords.
  4. State 4: Resolution / Fail-Safe Escalation: If confirmed, status becomes [RESOLVED]. If unresponsive within 4 seconds, state shifts directly to [EMERGENCY_ESCALATION].

05 Edge Cases & Cellular Dead-Zone Fallbacks

Trucks frequently pass through cellular weak zones. We engineered clear offline and timeout protocols:

  • 2-Ring Ringback Timeout: If the WebRTC socket does not handshake within 2.5 seconds, the server immediately triggers a high-frequency audible buzzer directly through the hardware speaker while pushing an urgent SMS alert.
  • False-Positive Tolerance: If an alert is triggered erroneously (e.g., driver wearing heavy dark sunglasses), the driver simply says "Theek hoon" ("I am fine"), and the state clears without stopping the vehicle.

07 PM Retrospective: Safety Demands Determinism

The most important lesson from this project is that safety-critical AI must never be left unconstrained. Combining cutting-edge voice models with rigid, deterministic state machines delivers the best of both worlds: natural human conversation on the front end, with guaranteed fail-safe guarantees on the back end.
