The PM Craft & Execution Vault

Anatomy of how I structure end-to-end product delivery — from root-cause discovery to UX flows, technical schemas, QA sanity, and rollout telemetry.

Phase 1: Problem Statement & Root-Cause Signal

Why are we building this? (The User & Business Friction)

Commercial fleet supervisors manage between 500 and 3,000 active assets. During field shadowing, I observed operators spending an average of 4.2 minutes per alert cross-referencing three separate UI tabs (Vehicle Location, Fuel Sensor Logs, Driver Rosters) to diagnose basic operational issues like idling or fuel drops. This lag delayed urgent route and safety interventions.

// Explicit Non-Goals (Scope Containment to Prevent Creep)
• We are NOT replacing historical raw-data XLSX exports in Phase 1.
• We are NOT permitting automated vehicle engine immobilization without dual human confirmation.
• We are NOT introducing arbitrary SQL input fields that could expose production databases.
Phase 2: UX Discovery & Operator Workflow Mapping

Eliminating Multi-Tab Cognitive Friction

Operators don't need a conversational chatbot that chats; they need actionable vehicle intelligence with immediate operational controls. I mapped the user journey from question to action:

Step 1: Intent Query
Operator speaks or types: "Show idle reefers in North Zone".
Step 2: Structured Cards
System renders condensed vehicle status cards with live telemetry tags.
Step 3: Direct Actions
Embedded CTA buttons: [Call Driver], [Ping Telematics], [Assign Route].
Phase 3: Technical Feasibility & Architecture Alignment

De-Risking Delivery: Protecting User Experience & Infrastructure

A responsive user experience requires dependable infrastructure. Rather than handing engineering a vague wishlist, I leveraged my CS background to evaluate data volume alongside tech leads. To prevent sluggish queries and database degradation, we defined clear Model Context Protocol (MCP) tool contracts over in-memory Redis caching—guaranteeing sub-second UI response times for operators.

Case Study Breakdown

Fleet AI Query Assistant & Telematics MCP

Product Scope

Commercial fleet managers monitor thousands of sensor data points per second. During field shadowing, I observed operators spending 4+ minutes cross-referencing multi-tab dashboards to diagnose basic idling alerts. I scoped an Agentic AI solution utilizing Model Context Protocol (MCP) and Redis to turn complex operational queries into instant natural-language responses.

Role: Product Manager
Company: Roadcast
Duration: Dec 2025 – Present
Impact: 75% faster diagnosis, 0% relational DB rate exhaustion

01 Problem Statement & Signal Discovery

Operators manage 500 to 3,000 active trucks. When an alert spikes (e.g. erratic idling or fuel anomaly), operators spent an average of 4.2 minutes switching between Vehicle Lists, Sensor Logs, and Shift Rosters. The root cause wasn't lack of data—it was high retrieval friction in a time-critical control room.

02 UX Discovery & Workflow Mapping

Interviewed 12 dispatchers and mapped their mental model. We realized dispatchers didn't want a freeform conversational chatbot—they needed immediate structured vehicle cards with embedded action controls (e.g. [Ping Telematics], [Call Driver], [Snooze Alert]). Designed low-friction interaction wireframes in Figma to eliminate multi-tab jumping.

03 Technical Scoping & Architecture

Rather than letting the LLM execute arbitrary SQL directly against TimescaleDB (which risked table locks and rate exhaustion), I worked with backend engineers to define specialized Model Context Protocol (MCP) tool schemas. The model translates user intent into pre-structured queries routed to a high-throughput Redis cache layer with short TTLs.

04 QA Sanity, Fail-safes & Edge Cases

Hardened the pipeline for edge conditions: (a) handling stale GPS packets in transit tunnels by falling back to the last known coordinate with a visual timestamp badge, (b) rate-limiting queries per dispatcher seat, and (c) preventing LLM hallucinations with strict Gherkin acceptance criteria in Jira.

05 Measurable Impact & Telemetry

  • Cut diagnostic turnaround time by 75% across participating fleet managers.
  • Maintained a P95 response latency under 1.5 seconds.
  • Eliminated primary database strain through pre-warmed Redis telemetry caching.

Autonomous Voice AI Driver Safety Intercom

Product Scope

Severe driver fatigue and drowsiness events require immediate verbal intervention. In a large fleet, human dispatchers cannot manually place calls within seconds of a sensor alert. I mapped user journeys and scoped the deterministic state-machine workflow for an automated voice alert POC.

Role: Product Manager
Company: Roadcast POC
Tech: LiveKit WebRTC, Sarvam AI, State Machines
Outcome: Sub-second automated driver intervention

01 Problem Statement & Safety Signal

Camera-based DMS alerts detect microsleeps, but mobile push notifications are easily ignored while driving at highway speeds. Waiting for a human operator to pick up the phone took over 90 seconds on average—far too late to prevent high-speed collisions.

02 UX Discovery & In-Cabin Context

A commercial truck cabin is noisy, and drivers speak colloquial vernacular languages (primarily Hindi). The user experience required zero visual interaction: immediate outbound voice calling that checks on the driver's alertness in natural spoken Hindi without requiring button presses.

03 Technical Implementation & State Machines

Paired WebRTC streaming (via LiveKit) with Sarvam AI's low-latency Indic speech models. Designed a deterministic state machine: ADAS Alert -> Outbound Call Initiated -> Speech Acknowledged -> Alert Cleared. If the driver answers and confirms alertness, the alert transitions to [RESOLVED].

04 QA Sanity & Fail-Safe Fallbacks

Critical safety guardrails: If the driver call does not connect within 2 rings (network failure) or the driver remains silent after two spoken prompts, the system immediately sends a high-priority SMS and escalates a red ticket directly to the 24/7 human operations desk for manual emergency dispatch.

05 Outcomes & Feasibility

Demonstrated a functional prototype delivering an automated voice safety check within <1.2 seconds of sensor alert trigger, proving the viability of autonomous safety workflows in commercial telematics.

Case Study Breakdown

Catalogue Cloud & SKU Variant Engine

Product Scope

Enterprise rollout of Catalogue Cloud for Reliance retail brands. Supported the onboarding of enterprise giants like Netmeds and JioMart by designing SKU relationship mapping, error detection rules, and QA test coverage matrices.

Role: Product Manager Intern
Company: Fynd (Reliance Group)
Duration: Aug – Nov 2024
Results: 3+ Enterprise clients live, -40% data errors

01 Problem Statement & Enterprise Friction

Onboarding massive pharmacy catalogs (Netmeds) involved hundreds of thousands of SKU combinations (dosages, packaging, brand variations). Mismatched attribute fields caused batch feed imports to fail silently or corrupt live catalog listings.

02 UX Discovery & Operations Friction

Catalog operation managers were spending entire days combing through obscure backend error logs. They needed an intuitive, proactive validation layer that surfaced exact column and value mismatches prior to batch ingestion.

03 Technical Scoping & Schema Mapping

Designed hierarchical parent-child SKU relations and strict schema validation rules across incoming JSON feeds. Ensured attribute inheritance so variant-level updates didn't overwrite brand-level metadata.

04 QA Sanity & Test Coverage

Authored exhaustive edge-case test matrices covering circular variant references, missing mandatory regulatory drug tags, and special-character sanitization in bulk CSV uploads, reducing QA cycle times by 30%.

05 Measurable Impact

Successfully supported the onboarding of Netmeds, JioMart, and Milkbasket, slashing catalog sync error rates by 30-40% and accelerating brand launch timelines.

Case Study Breakdown

LinkedIn Mentorship Network & UX Teardown

Product Scope

A comprehensive 0-to-1 product discovery project tackling cold-start networking friction on LinkedIn. Conducted 25+ user interviews and built an interactive Figma prototype for structured 15-minute micro-mentorship sprints.

Focus: Discovery & UX Craft
Interviews: 25+ Participants
Prototype: High-Fidelity Figma
Usability: 80% Unassisted Task Completion

01 Problem Statement & Signal

Over 95% of cold mentorship messages on LinkedIn go unanswered. Both mentors and mentees expressed mutual frustration: seekers felt ignored, while mentors felt overwhelmed by vague requests to "pick your brain".

02 UX Discovery & Behavioral Insights

Synthesized 25+ interviews into clear personas. Key discovery: Mentors are actually very willing to help early-career individuals, but they fear open-ended time waste. Bounding the commitment to a structured 15-minute format removes 90% of the cognitive barrier.

03 Product Architecture & Solution Design

Designed 15-minute "Micro-Mentorship Sprints" with mandatory agenda selection (e.g. Resume Feedback, Mock PM Interview, Career Pivot Advice), automated Google Calendar syncing, and mutual accountability ratings.

04 Usability Testing & Refinements

Conducted usability testing sessions with 10 participants on Figma. Identified drop-offs during calendar slot booking and revised the UI to display 3 immediate one-click availability chips instead of a dense monthly picker.

05 Product Insights & Learnings

Demonstrated that reducing ambiguity through structured constraints is the single most powerful lever for converting passive community goodwill into active mentorship interactions.
